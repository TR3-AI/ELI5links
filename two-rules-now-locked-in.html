Two rules, now locked in 🔒

You asked for both to be enforceable this time — and sent as a link, not a wall of text. Here they are, and where I wrote them.

📱 1
Over 350 characters → a link, always

Any reply to you longer than 350 characters must become an ELI5 page with a short link — never a long chat message, no “just this once.” This very page is that rule doing its job.

⬇️
🔍 2
“Done” now means done AND built to the plan

The old habit: build it, then you find out it was built wrong. The new rule: plan questions get raised BEFORE building, not after. And nobody’s “done” is taken on their word.

⬇️
👨‍🔬 3
What the manager must do now

When a developer or tester says “done,” the manager checks two things first-hand — against the real code AND the plan: (1) is it truly finished? (2) was it built to the plan and instructions? If the plan was unclear, that question had to go up before any code was written.

⬇️
🔎 4
What I (Ops) must do now

I never take the manager’s word that it’s done or on-plan. Before it reaches you, I open the plan myself and check the built thing matches it. If it doesn’t, I don’t tell you “done” — I send it back down and tell you what’s off. This is the part that was missing.

⬇️
📜 5
Where I wrote it (so it can’t be skipped)

Both rules are now in the shared rule book that Ops and every manager, developer, and tester load at startup — one place, so it can’t drift. I also hardened the manager rules and deleted the old line that said “nobody re-checks the manager.” Now I do.

Short version: long answers become links, and nothing is “done” until it’s checked against the plan — by the manager, then by me. Written into 3 rule files, effective now.
