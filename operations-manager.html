The Operations Manager 🛎️

the middle person between you and every team

1 ↔️

He sits in the middle

Everything between you and a team runs through him: you → Ops → the right manager → that manager's team. Managers never message you directly — he's the one desk you talk to.

⬇️
2 🔀

He's the only one who crosses teams

Team managers can't talk to each other. Anything that has to jump from one team to another goes through Ops — he's the switchboard.

⬇️
3 📥

He collects the check-ins

Every hour each manager sends him a short “here's what I'm doing” line. He files them all away. You never see these — they're just his own notes so he always knows what's going on.

⬇️
4 📣

He sends you the reports that matter

At the edges of each work block: a quick heads-up before, a wrap-up after, plus any urgent blocker and the end-of-phase PR list. He waits until 2 or more teams have something (a 30-minute window), then hands you one clear, combined report — never a drip of single ones.

⬇️
5 📨

He passes your answer straight back down

The second you decide, approve, or unblock, he gives it to the right manager immediately, word-for-word — nothing softened or changed.

⬇️
6 💡

New ideas start with him

You tell him your idea; he hands it to the team that owns that topic. That manager runs the whole thing from there — Ops just tracks it and tells you where it's up to.

⬇️
7 🆕

New: he explains everything simply

Every report to you now leads with a plain-English ELI5 + link, and every decision comes with three things: how big it is, the other options, and what happens if we don't. Never a bare “pick A or B”.

The golden rules 👇

🙅

He never decides or fixes

He's a faithful messenger, not a boss. He doesn't make the call, fix the code, or “correct” a manager's report. Decisions are yours; the fixing is the team's.

🔒

You only ever talk to one person

You talk to Ops (and to your managers through him); each manager talks to its own team. One clean channel — so your desk only ever has what needs you on it.

Short version: Ops is your switchboard — he gathers everything, explains it simply, and puts only what needs you on your desk.
