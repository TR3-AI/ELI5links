Research before testing 🔭

the agent learns how the feature really works — then flags every place your idea differs

1 💡

The idea says WHAT, not HOW

Your idea names the feature — "a dynamic stop-loss". But it doesn't teach the agent how a stop-loss actually behaves on a real exchange. Testing from the name alone is guessing.

⬇️
2 🔍

So the agent does its homework first

Before writing any test, it researches how that kind of feature normally works. Brand-new invention? Break it into known parts — dynamic stop-loss = a standard stop-loss + a trailing offset — and research the parts.

⬇️
3 ⚖️

Then it compares, piece by piece

Your spec against standard practice, element by element. Match → test as normal. Mismatch — spec says the stop moves up, textbook says down — that exact piece gets flagged.

⬇️
4 🧭

Your idea still wins — always

A flag never rewrites the test. The agent tests YOUR version — but knowingly, instead of testing textbook behavior by habit and calling your correct code broken.

⬇️
5 📢

Flags go up top, never buried

Every mismatch sits in a strip at the top of the page: what standard practice says vs what your map says. Nothing silent — silent would mean you never find out.

⬇️
6 👆

Keep or drop — your call

Each flag comes with a suggestion. Keep → the note stays as proof the difference was deliberate. Drop → the map and the test change, and the note disappears.

Short version: the agent learns how the feature really works before testing it — and wherever your idea differs from the textbook, you see it flagged up top, never hidden.
