Found it: why the robot went 0-for-67 🔎

The exact cause, proven with numbers. Written down as issue #101.

1 🚚

The mail room fell behind

Every price update that arrives must first be written into 4 notebooks (one per run, 5–8) before the next update is even looked at. During busy game hours, the waiting line grew to minutes.

⬇️
2 ⏱️

The 60-second rule threw everything away

A safety rule says: a price older than 60 seconds when finally read is untrustworthy — bin it. The robot was reading prices 2 to 13 minutes late (I measured it in the logs). So it binned basically every price, all night.

⬇️
3 🔁

The doom loop kept it broken

After a hiccup, a market must show a fresh “proof photo” to be trusted again. But proof photos sat in the same slow line — read late, binned, new photo ordered, repeat. 961 times in 45 minutes. Trust never came back, so the betting brain saw no prices at all — and 67 good AI answers died priceless.

⬇️
4 📚

Why the line was so long

We watch 2,523 markets at once — and about 373 are long-dead old games (issue #100) that still flood the mail room with paperwork for nothing.

⬇️
5 💡

Your 2% question: fact, not hallucination

It's real and it's in the code: the robot only bets when the AI's chance beats the market price by at least 2 points. Example: AI says 75%, price is 72¢ → edge 3% → bet. Price at 74¢ → edge 1% → no bet. Runs 1–4 had the exact same rule — it is not what changed, and it rejected nothing this run (nothing ever reached it).

⬇️
6 🔧

The fix, in 3 parts (before any rerun)

1. Stop watching dead markets — only today's games (~7× less mail).
2. Hand fresh prices to the betting brain immediately; write the 4 notebooks in the background.
3. Break the doom loop — a binned proof photo must not instantly order another one.
