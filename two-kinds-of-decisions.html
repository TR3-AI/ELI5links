Two kinds of decisions 🛣️

which come straight to you, which come through Ops

1 ⌚

You get asked to decide at two moments

Some decisions come up while an idea is still being planned. Others pop up later, while a project is being built. Those two travel by different roads — here's which.

⬇️
2 📝

Planning decisions → straight to you

While a new idea is being shaped into a plan: which language, what it should do, the budget, the plan drafts, the Wayfinder map, the final go-ahead. These come directly from the manager to you — Ops is not in the loop. You two are inventing it together, so you talk face to face.

⬇️
3 🏗️

Everyday decisions → through Ops

Once a project is being built, smaller calls come up: a blocker, “merge this or try once more?”, an approval mid-build. These reach you through Ops, who gathers them from every team.

⬇️
4 💡

Why two roads?

Planning is you and one manager shaping one thing — direct is simplest. Building is many teams running at once — so Ops collects their questions and hands you one tidy batch, instead of every team pinging you all day.

The easy way to remember 👇

⭐

Making it vs running it

Making the plan? → straight to you.
Running the plan? → through Ops.

🏠

Same idea, house version

Designing the house — rooms, budget, blueprint, the final yes — is you and your architect, direct. Once the builders start, on-site questions reach you through the site manager (Ops), so you're not stopped by every crew.

Short version: deciding what to build is you + the manager, direct. Little calls while building come through Ops.
