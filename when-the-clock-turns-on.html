When the clock turns on ⏰

it only runs when teams are actually working — never pokes an idle team

1 ⏰

The clock has a gate

It doesn’t just tick all day. It only runs when there’s real work happening — so it never nags a team that’s sitting idle.

⬇️
2 👥

On only when 2+ teams are busy

The rule: the clock runs only when at least two teams have live work — an idea has come in and the manager has started handing it out. Fewer than two → off.

⬇️
3 🗣️

The team wakes it up

The second a manager takes an idea and starts assigning work, it pings me: “Received an idea — start your clock.” That’s the on-switch — nothing starts until a team says it’s working.

⬇️
4 ✅

Two live → I start it

Once two teams have pinged, I start the clock — and it only checks in with the teams actually working. Idle teams are left alone (they’d have no clue what I meant).

⬇️
5 😴

Work stops → clock stands down

When a team finishes or goes quiet it tells me, and I stand its clock down. Back under two busy teams and the clock switches off on its own.

⬇️
6 🚫

Right now: off

All three teams are idle this second, so the clock is off — on purpose. Kick an idea to two teams and it comes alive by itself. Nothing for you to switch.

Short version: the clock only runs when 2+ teams are truly working. Teams flip it on by telling me they’ve started, and off when they stop. No idle team ever gets nagged. It’s written into the rulebook and saved to GitHub, and passed to every manager.
