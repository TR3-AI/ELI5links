The whole map 🗺️

how everything works — your idea to shipped

1 🧠

One Manager, one desk

You talk to one Manager. It never builds or codes — it plans, routes the work, checks it, and reports. Everyone else answers to it, not you.

⬇️
2 👥

The team = 10

A core team is 10 specialists, each in its own pane and its own AI model: manager, 2 planners, research, a tester, 2 spec-writers, 2 backend builders, and a front-end. You can run any number of core teams (projects) at once.

⬇️
3 💡

Your idea → a costed plan

You bring an idea. The Manager picks the best language with you, names the repo, and has research price the tools. Nothing starts until you OK the cost.

⬇️
4 ✍️

You decide — four times

Two rounds, two approvals each. Round 1: you OK a fact sheet, then the plan → into the Wayfinder flow where you make every call. Round 2: a second fact sheet, then the final plan. Four yeses before any code.

⬇️
5 📚

Plan → spec → job cards

The approved plan becomes a PRD, then a skeleton, then a full spec, then small tickets — each mapped to exactly where it fits.

⬇️
6 🔴

Build: red → green → tidy

Backend is test-first: one builder writes a failing test (what “done” means), another makes it pass, the first tidies up. Repeat per card.

⬇️
7 ⭐

The robot gate

Cards ship in PRs of ≤5. A robot (Greptile) scores each out of 5. 5/5 auto-merges; below that: 7 retries → split → still stuck → your call.

⬇️
8 🚦

Two lanes: staging vs main

Work lands in staging (the rehearsal lane) on its own. Going live to main always needs a passing test and your explicit yes — the core team owns that door, nothing self-promotes.

⬇️
9 🏢

Big jobs = departments

For a large project the plan is split into sections. Each is built by a temporary 8-agent department (front-end always in, idle if no screens). A team runs its core + up to 2 departments at once (3 crews). No limit on how many separate core teams run. Departments never talk to each other or to you.

⬇️
10 🔀

How a department ships

Each department builds on its own branch — that's its own staging. Tickets merge there at 5/5. When the whole department is green it opens one PR into the one central staging. The whole project is tested together there — and that test is the one the main gate needs.

⬇️
11 🗣️

Ops, the switchboard

The Operations Manager sits between you and every crew. It batches so you're never pinged by one crew — it waits for 2+ crews (any team or department). Nothing idles: after 30 minutes with no reply a crew moves to pre-approved work — except a menu question, which waits for you.

⬇️
12 🗂️

Memory on GitHub, never local

Every manager, team, and department — Ops included — keeps its notes in its own GitHub repo, committed and pushed as it goes. A local-only copy is a bug, not a save. Nothing is lost to a crash or a new session.

The golden rules 👇

🙅

Never decides, never guesses

The Manager never decides or fixes (that's you and the team), never polls (crews ping it the moment they finish), and never trusts “done” on a word — it re-checks the real commit, test count, and score.

Short version: one Manager runs a 10-person core team; you approve the plan (four times) and the go-live; robots grade the code; big jobs fan out into 8-agent departments (core + up to 2) that each build on their own branch and roll up into one staging → main, your call.
