The whole map 🗺️

how everything works — your idea to shipped

1 🧠

One Manager, one desk

You talk to one Manager. It never builds or codes — it plans the job, routes the work, checks it, and reports. Everyone else answers to it, not to you.

⬇️
2 👥

The team

A row of specialist workers, each in its own pane and its own AI model: 2 planners, a researcher, spec-writers, backend builders, a front-end, and a tester.

⬇️
3 💡

Your idea → a costed plan

You bring an idea. The Manager picks the best language with you, names the repo, and has research price the tools. Nothing starts until you OK the cost.

⬇️
4 ✍️

You decide — twice

Planners draft it → you approve a fact sheet → you walk the Wayfinder flow making every call → a second fact sheet → you approve again. Two yeses before any code.

⬇️
5 📚

Plan → spec → job cards

The approved plan becomes a PRD, then a skeleton, then a full spec, then small tickets — each mapped to exactly where it fits.

⬇️
6 🔴

Build: red → green → tidy

Backend is test-first: one builder writes a failing test (what “done” means), another makes it pass, the first tidies up. Repeat per card.

⬇️
7 ⭐

The robot gate

Cards ship in PRs of ≤5. A robot (Greptile) scores each out of 5. 5/5 auto-merges to staging; below that: 7 retries → split → still stuck → your call.

⬇️
8 🚦

Two lanes: staging vs main

Everything lands in staging (the rehearsal lane) on its own. Going live to main always needs a passing test and your explicit yes. The Manager never promotes itself.

⬇️
9 🏢

Big jobs = departments

For large plans, the work splits into sections — each built by a small temporary 8-person team (front-end always in). Up to 3 teams at once. Departments never talk to each other or to you directly.

⬇️
10 🗣️

The switchboard

The Operations Manager sits between you and every team: gathers everything, explains it simply, and puts only what needs you on your desk.

⬇️
11 🗂️

Memory that survives

The Manager keeps private notes — state, progress, decisions — in its own separate repo, saved as it goes. A crash or a new session loses nothing.

The golden rules 👇

🙅

Never decides, never guesses

The Manager never decides or fixes (that's you and the team), never polls (workers ping it the moment they finish), and never trusts “done” on a word — it re-checks the real commit, test count, and score.

Short version: one Manager runs the team; you approve the plan and the go-live; robots grade the code; big jobs fan out into small department teams that all report up through Ops.
