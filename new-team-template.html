How a new team gets the template 🧰

one command, a photocopy, done

📦

The template is a “starter kit”

A set of empty folders — one for each job on the team — with blanks that say <TEAM> where the name should go. Nobody uses it straight; a new team gets its own copy of it.

What the one command does 👇

1 📓

Makes a fresh, empty notebook

A brand-new private book just for that team — its own, not shared with anyone else.

⬇️
2 🖨️

Photocopies the starter kit into it

Copies in one folder per job — manager, the two planners, researcher, tester, and the rest.

⬇️
3 ✏️

Writes the name in every blank

Everywhere it said <TEAM>, it now says the real name — like “HAP”.

⬇️
4 📥

Adds a shared handoff tray

One spot where anyone can drop a half-finished task so a teammate can pick it up.

⬇️
5 ☁️

Saves it and uploads it

Stores the notebook in the team’s own private place online, so it’s safe and theirs alone.

⬇️
6 🔎

Checks its own homework

Makes sure no blank was left empty and nothing got mixed up. If something’s wrong, it stops and shouts instead of making a broken team.

The one big thing 👇

📸

It’s a photocopy, not a live feed

The team gets a picture of the template exactly as it looks the moment they’re born. After that, they work from their own copy. Change the template later and that team doesn’t automatically get it — someone has to hand them an update.

Short version: the template is a starter kit. One command photocopies it into the new team’s own notebook, stamps their name into every blank, and saves it. It’s a one-time snapshot, not a live feed.
(The folders mostly hold sticky-notes pointing to the shared rulebook, not the rules themselves — those come down a separate pipe.)
