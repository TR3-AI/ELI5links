Who grades the work?
❓

the one who wrote the checklist — or the manager?

📝

Someone writes the “done” checklist. Someone grades the work against it. The question: should those be the same person, or two different people?

IDEA A ✍️

The writer also grades it

The ticket-writer grades their own tickets; the two planners grade their own phase. “You wrote the bar, so you hold people to it.”

IDEA B 💼

The manager grades it

One person — the manager — grades every job and every batch, against the checklist and the plan.

⬇️
👉

My pick: Idea B — the manager

🛠️The ticket-writer also helped build the job (wrote its test, fixed the code). If they grade it too, they’re grading their own work.
📏One grader = one steady, fair standard for everything. Lots of people grading their own work gets wobbly and uneven.
🔓A good checklist can be graded by anyone. If only the writer can tell it’s done, the checklist was too fuzzy — so this keeps checklists clear.
✍️The writer still holds people to account — by writing the bar. That’s their power. Grading is just a separate job.
⬇️
✅

So, in one line

The writer sets the bar → the manager grades against it → nobody grades their own work.

One honest note: for a whole phase it’s a closer call — the planners didn’t build it, so it’s not self-marking. I still say the manager, because they actually ran the phase and know it first-hand.
This is my pick — your call. See also: who writes the list 📋
