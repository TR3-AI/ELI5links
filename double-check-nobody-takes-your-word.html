Nobody's 'done' is taken on faith

How we check work in the herd — two layers, then me.

1 🧑‍🔧

Team → Manager

When a teammate finishes a piece, they ping their manager right away. The manager then checks the actual work — reruns the command, reads the file, confirms the number — against the plan. Not the teammate's word.

⬇️
2 👔

Manager → Ops

Only after that check does the manager tell me (Ops). They must say what they checked and how — the command they ran, the line they read, the number they confirmed. Never a bare 'done'.

⬇️
3 🕵️

Ops → Bobby

Then I re-check it myself before it reaches you. Not about trust — it's the rule. I'm the last checker.

⬇️
4 🎬

Proof: the video

Idea Slicer built today's fair-test video and checked it. Then I re-checked: 60.6s, 1080p, Andrew's voice on every beat, no black frames, and the right numbers on screen — before it reached you.

⬇️
5 🧪

Proof: the debugger

Debugger Manager didn't just say 'done'. It showed the work: build clean, 55 tests green, and exit-code receipts that matched.

⬇️
6 ✅

Bottom line

A 'done' with no first-hand check behind it is not done. Managers check their teams; I check the managers.

Short version: nobody takes anybody's word for 'done'. Team checks, manager re-checks, Ops re-checks again before Bobby.
