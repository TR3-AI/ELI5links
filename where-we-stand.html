Where we stand 🧭

The whole build in one look — done, live, one thing left.

1 🚪

One door in

You talk to one Manager, nobody else. It runs the whole team, routes the work, checks it, and reports back — it never bogs you down with the small stuff.

⬇️
2 ✅

Four yeses before any code

Two planners draft the plan. You approve four times (two fact sheets + two plans) before a single line is built. Nothing sneaks past you.

⬇️
3 🏗️

Built the safe way

Every feature is test-first (write the failing test, make it pass, tidy up). A robot scores each batch out of 5; 5/5 slides into staging. Going live to the real shelf still needs your yes.

⬇️
4 👥

Big jobs split up

Work fans out into lean departments (a core team + up to 2 at a time, as many core teams as you want). Each builds on its own branch, then it all rolls back into one place to be tested together.

⬇️
5 🗂️

Everyone remembers, nobody spams you

Every team and seat keeps its own memory on GitHub. The Operations Manager sits between you and the teams and batches the updates, so you're never pinged by one crew at a time.

⬇️
6 🚀

Built, live — one thing left

All of the above is done and live today; every team inherits it, including the 10th seat, the tester (seated but idle). The only piece still parked — on purpose — is Mr. Tester's testing pipeline. Your move.

Short version: the whole delegation system is built and live — one Manager, four approvals, test-first with a robot gate, departments that roll into one place, memory on GitHub, and an Operations Manager batching your updates. Only the tester's pipeline is left.
