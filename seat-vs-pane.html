Seat vs pane 🪑

two different things — they pair up. Same idea for departments.

😊

The one-liner

Seat = the chair (the job on the team).
Pane = the screen the worker sits at and types in.
Building a team puts one worker-in-a-pane in each chair.

⬇️
1 🪑

A seat is the chair

It's just the definition of a role on the team — this job, this model, this task. It lives in the team's roster list. On its own it's a name on paper, not a running thing.

⬇️
2 🖥️

A pane is the screen

It's the live window where that worker actually sits and works. Real, running, doing (or waiting for) a job.

⬇️
3 🏗️

Building the team joins the two

Standing up a team is what turns seats into panes: it opens one pane per seat and labels it "team + seat" (e.g. "DMS Front-end"). A full core team is built one way; a department is built the lean way — same idea, fewer chairs.

⬇️
4 🎨

So “the front-end seat is always there” means…

The front-end chair is always on the team. Once the team is built it also gets a pane — but that pane sits idle (does nothing) until you've agreed a design, and stays idle on any job with no UI at all. The chair is there; the worker just waits.

⬇️
5 📍

One honest catch for DMS right now

A chair being on the roster doesn't mean its screen is open yet. DMS today has only 3 live panes — Manager, the backend one, and Research. The other seats are on the roster but not opened yet. So right now, for DMS, "seat" and "pane" don't fully line up until those are staffed.

⬇️
6 🏢

Departments — exactly the same idea

A department is a small, temporary team = its own tab with one pane per seat (the lean 8: manager, research, tester, sdd-skeleton, full-sdd, feature-tickets, green-pass, front-end).
So "front-end is always seated" in a department = the front-end chair is always one of the 8 and gets a pane when the department is built — it just idles if that department has no UI.
Departments are temporary (spun up for one slice of a big plan, closed on your say-so — the panes close, the work and branches stay), and at most core + 2 departments run at once.

Short version: a seat is the chair (a role on the team); a pane is the live screen the worker sits at. Standing up a team — core or department — opens one pane per chair. The front-end chair is always there; its screen just waits until there's a design / some UI.
