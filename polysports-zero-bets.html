Why the robot made 0 bets 🤖

24 hours, 95 questions, 67 good answers — and not one bet. Here's why.

1 🤖

The robot did its job

It watched live games all day and night and asked the AI “who wins?” 95 times. It got 67 clear, usable answers back. So far, so good.

⬇️
2 🏷️

Every bet needs a price tag

Before betting, the robot must see the market's current price to know if the bet is worth it. No price tag = no bet — that's a safety rule, on purpose.

⬇️
3 📺

The price-tag TV was broken

Runs 5–8 read prices from a live stream — like a TV channel that shows prices. That channel kept crashing and wiping itself: 961 failures in just 45 minutes of logs, and over the night 147,117 “no signal” notes against only 683 real price updates.

⬇️
4 🚫

All 67 chances died the same way

I read every rejection line. All 67 say the same thing: “no price available” for every team in the game. Not one was rejected for a low score or a small edge.

⬇️
5 ✅

Your rules were NOT the problem

The 80/60/55 bars, the $5,000 volume rule, the score floor, the 2% edge rule — all identical to runs 1–4. None of them ever voted. They never got a price to judge.

⬇️
6 🔁

Why runs 1–4 traded and this one didn't

Old runs simply asked the website “price, please?” each time — a quick question, almost always answered. New runs must watch the TV channel instead — stricter and safer, but the channel is broken. Likely why: we tuned it to 1,936 markets, including ~373 dead old ones (issue #100).
