“done” list? 📋

who sets the bar — and who checks it

📋

What the “done” list is

The checklist that decides a job is really finished — not just “I think I’m done.” It spells out exactly what must be true before we call it done.

⬇️
✍️

Who writes it?

The list is written before the work starts, by the planning side — never the person who’ll do the job. You don’t get to write your own test.

🎫One job (a “ticket”) → the ticket-writer writes its list. They turn the big design into a small job with a clear “it’s done when…”
📦A whole batch (a “phase”) → the two planners write its list. They own the big plan, so they say what a whole batch must deliver.
⬇️
🏠

Some boxes never change

Tests pass, work saved, passed its grade (the PR gate), nothing left over — these are the house rules on every list. Nobody rewrites them.

⬇️
🔍

Who checks it?

The manager — for both the small job and the whole batch. They check the work against the list AND the big plan, so it did everything asked and stayed in bounds.

⬇️
⚖️

Who checks the manager? Nobody needs to

The plan and the list are the fixed ruler. The manager is measured by them — so there’s no endless “who checks the checker.”

⬇️
✅

Nobody marks their own homework

Three different hands: one writes the bar (✍️), one does the work (🛠️), one checks it (🔍). The doer never sets or grades its own bar.

The only bit that stays with you: the final “yes, ship it” to main 🚀 — a go decision, not a “done” check.
