The debugger & autofix — why they're parked 🅿️

good questions — here's the honest answer

🔍

A debugger doesn't fix bugs

It's a magnifying glass: it helps find where and why code broke. It never writes the fix. So parking it doesn't leave anything unfixed.

⬇️
🤖

The coding AI is the fixer

When the watcher finds a red, it hands the finding to the coding AI and keeps nudging until it's green — and blocks any push or pull request while it's still red. That loop is the fix path (steps 3, 6, 7).

The debugger

🧰

It's not removed from OMP

OMP's debugger still works — you can open CodeLLDB by hand any time. The plan just doesn't auto-run it in version 1.

⬇️
🎯

v1 already tells you where

The new test runner names the exact test that crashed or hung, and full crash traces come free. That covers most of what an auto-debugger would report.

⬇️
💥

Auto-running it fights the watcher

The watcher kills a frozen run's whole process tree; an attached debugger clashes with that. A replay is best-effort — a flaky bug may not repeat, and it may stop at the symptom, not the cause. It never changes pass/fail — it's only advice.

Autofix

🔌

Off, not deleted

Autofix isn't gone — it's switched off until it's made safe.

⬇️
⚠️

Why off: it's too eager

Today it runs fixers across your whole project, unbounded, and hides its own errors — and it can collide with the coding AI editing the same files. In a tool built for honest, tamper-proof checks, a fixer that makes broad hidden edits is a risk.

✅

Both parked, not deleted

They're set aside to build after the core catch-and-block loop and the safety are solid — the plan says to revisit both. Want either in version 1? Say the word and I'll have the plan changed to build it now.
